# Thesis: Interfaces After the Chatbot

For two decades, software offered two species of interface: **dashboards** and **dialogues**. Dashboards optimized for monitoring the aggregate; chatbots for impersonating a person. Both broke the moment AI moved from answering to *acting*.

Dashboards fail because they assume the operator decides what matters. They render a thousand rows because they don't know which one matters — so operators mine signal from noise. Chatbots fail in the opposite direction: they collapsed interaction back into a text box and asked users to *describe* what they want, as if speech — not the decision — were the primitive.

The AI-native interface starts from the **decision**, not the data or the dialog. Infer first, then render: one card, one choice, one audit record. The model does what models do well — cheap, parallel judgment — and the human keeps the veto, the rephrase, and the consequence. Latency moved from the interface to the inference, where it belongs.

The real trust problem is not "the bot answered," it is "the bot acted." Confidence is not a widget; it is a **routing contract**: high confidence buys autonomy inside an undo window, mid confidence buys a proposal you must approve, low confidence buys nothing but a transparent invitation to judge. Time becomes the permission system — the ten-second undo proves that flow can be reversible instead of trust-first.

Two years out: every tool will be a stream of decisions with attached reasoning, not a pile of rows or a chat thread. The dashboard becomes the audit log. The chatbot becomes the relay race we no longer need to run. The least possible interface around the most capable model is not a constraint — it is the design program.

**One decision at a time.**
